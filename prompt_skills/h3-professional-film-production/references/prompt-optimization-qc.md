# H3 prompt optimization and quality control

Compile for instruction salience, not maximum word count.

## Priority stack

Resolve instructions in this order:

1. authoritative story, identity and reference relationships;
2. selected H3 mode and generation-profile limits;
3. opening state and continuity inheritance;
4. dominant dramatic/performance/action change;
5. camera responsibility and decisive visibility;
6. endpoint and handoff;
7. dialogue/sound timing;
8. lighting, color and material changes;
9. secondary atmosphere and narrow failure guards.

When load is too high, remove or split lower-priority demands. Never solve overload by making every instruction shorter but equally competing.

## Compilation test

For every phrase ask:

- Is it observable in this framing and duration?
- Does it alter output or repeat an existing authority?
- Is it stable global truth, local change or metadata?
- Does it conflict with another instruction or reference?
- Does it require an unplanned transition?
- Does it compete with the dominant action or camera responsibility?
- Is its timing/order clear?

Delete or relocate phrases that fail.

## Natural-language structure

Use stable subject names and chronological clauses. Bind action to subject, trigger and result. Bind camera to a viewing responsibility. Bind sound/light/effects to sources and timing. Bind references to exact responsibilities and exclusions.

Prefer:

```text
On hearing the latch, she stops before turning; the camera holds until her eyes find the door, then makes one restrained push and settles before she speaks.
```

Avoid:

```text
cinematic, emotional, dynamic motion, dramatic camera, ultra detailed, masterpiece
```

## Negative constraints

Use a short targeted set derived from actual risk: identity drift, duplicate subject/prop, wrong speaker, repeated dialogue, broken contact, reversed direction, endpoint mismatch, illegible required text or continuity reset. Prefer positive state contracts. Avoid generic mega-lists that dilute the target action.

## Contradiction scan

Reject or reconcile:

- static and moving camera in the same phase;
- mutually exclusive gaze/body/contact states;
- global style versus local light/color conflict;
- reference pose versus required blocking;
- FL2VA endpoint versus described end state;
- exact continuation versus reset/re-establish wording;
- silent interval versus early dialogue;
- shallow focus versus required multi-plane action;
- LoRA role/trigger conflict;
- more actions, lines or effects than duration can show.

## Final scoring gate

Score each segment `0–2` on:

- dramatic causality;
- character/identity clarity;
- performance observability;
- spatial/action readability;
- camera motivation;
- visual-design coherence;
- audiovisual timing;
- reference/LoRA correctness;
- endpoint/continuity strength;
- provider/schema legality.

Require no zero and a minimum total of 16/20. A zero is `BLOCK`; revise the responsible layer before compilation. Do not increase score with decorative detail.

## Revision discipline

When optimizing one segment, preserve unaffected global facts, other segment ranges, references and approved text. State the causal weakness fixed. Do not rewrite the entire timeline for stylistic novelty.
