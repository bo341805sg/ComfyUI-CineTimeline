# Distilled director core

## Segment by dramatic and production logic

Create a new segment when a boundary materially changes the dramatic objective/tactic/result, location or topology, dominant action phase, viewpoint responsibility, required reference interface, or safe generation load.

Keep one segment when changes are phases of one continuous action and the camera can preserve geography, visibility and timing. Every segment needs a beginning state, trigger, development, consequence and settled handoff.

## Design professional visual style

Translate mood into a coherent image system rather than prestige adjectives:

- format/genre and degree of realism;
- composition grammar and negative-space behavior;
- palette relationships, saturation and contrast curve;
- motivated key/fill/practical sources and direction;
- time/weather atmosphere and volumetric behavior;
- surface/material response, skin and fabric treatment;
- depth staging, focus behavior and movement texture;
- restrained finishing such as grain or highlight roll-off.

State what remains constant globally and what changes for the current beat. Avoid internally incompatible style and camera instructions unless deliberately motivated.

## Design characters for performance and consistency

Separate immutable identity anchors; baseline costume anchors; current posture, weight, breath, tension, gaze, facial action, gesture and tempo; and state changes such as wetness, dirt, injury, prop ownership or costume damage.

Use references or declared LoRA metadata as identity authority. Do not invent trigger words. Do not restate every facial detail during action; use the stable character ID and describe changing performance evidence.

For full acting design, apply [screen-performance.md](screen-performance.md). Keep this file focused on the directing system; do not duplicate the performance reference into every prompt.

## Block actors and props

For each meaningful movement define:

```text
start mark -> trigger -> path -> obstacle/contact -> end mark -> relationship result -> settle
```

Protect orientation, gaze, silhouettes, hands, prop ownership, support/balance, force direction, entry/exit and screen direction. Movement must change action possibility, relationship, information, attention or power.

For contact, specify approach, contact point, grip/support, force direction, weight transfer, response and release/settle. Do not jump from approach directly to completed contact.

## Design shots and camera choreography

Choose viewpoint to establish geography, read performance, reveal/conceal information, protect contact/action, express a relationship shift, or provide an editable transition.

Define start framing, camera side, height, distance/lens character, focus target, movement trigger, path or hold, speed behavior, reveal/reframe, end framing and settle. Separate actor motion from camera motion. Use a static witness when movement adds no information or emotional access. Avoid stacking orbit, dolly, crane, zoom and rack focus into one short segment.

## Preserve continuity

At every boundary compare narrative knowledge and affect; identity/wardrobe/posture/gaze; mark/depth/axis/eyeline/direction; body support/action phase/contact/force; prop owner/hand/orientation/state; camera side/framing/motion/focus; light/weather/material response; dialogue/voice/ambience/sound bridge; and editorial handles.

Classify a difference as match, motivated visible/audible change, approved expressive discontinuity, tolerable variation, continuity error or insufficient evidence. Never infer an absent transition.

## Direct sound

Design a continuous ambience bed and perspective, subject Foley, discrete story events, dialogue/vocal behavior, deliberate silence, optional non-diegetic music, and an outgoing tail/bridge. Sound must support geography and causality. Do not reset ambience at each segment or fill every moment with music.
