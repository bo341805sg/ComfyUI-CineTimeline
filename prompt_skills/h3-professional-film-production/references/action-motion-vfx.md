# Action, motion and effects direction

Design motion as readable cause and effect. Use the lowest complexity that preserves story impact.

## Universal motion phases

For body, object, vehicle and effects motion specify:

```text
initial state -> trigger -> anticipation/preparation -> initiation -> travel/change -> interaction/impact -> response -> recovery -> settle/handoff
```

Protect mass, balance, support, inertia, momentum, force direction, collision/contact and environmental response. Do not teleport between key poses.

## Human locomotion and contact

State support foot, weight direction, path, pace, obstacle, reach, grip/contact, recipient response and end balance when relevant. Keep faces/hands/contact visible at the decisive phase.

For fights, falls, lifts, embraces, dance or stunts, separate the dominant exchange into phases and preserve safety/consent/story intent. Avoid several independent contacts in a short segment. Use a simpler silhouette-readable fallback when anatomy or occlusion risk is high.

## Vehicles, animals and crowds

- Vehicles: define lane/path, speed behavior, steering/braking cause, suspension/body response, wheel/ground interaction, camera relation and safe endpoint.
- Animals/creatures: respect anatomy, gait, weight and species behavior; distinguish character intent from locomotion.
- Crowds: define collective flow, density, dominant directional behavior and a small number of hero actions; avoid individually directing every background person.

## Fabric, hair, weather and simulation

Tie secondary motion to source forces and material properties: wind direction/strength, acceleration, gravity, impact, water flow, cloth weight/stiffness, hair length/texture and damping. Keep secondary motion subordinate to the primary action.

## VFX and transformations

Define:

- source and trigger;
- spatial origin, scale, path and interaction surface;
- phase progression and visible causal mechanism;
- light, shadow, reflection, occlusion and material response;
- subject reaction and environmental consequence;
- endpoint and residue;
- what remains practical/photographic versus transformed.

For transformations, preserve anchor geometry and stage intermediate states. Avoid a one-step morph when identity or material continuity matters.

## Risk reduction

Flag shots with multi-body contact, complex handoffs, severe occlusion, fast topology change, continuous-camera choreography, dense crowds, fluid/destruction or incompatible endpoints. Simplify action, split the segment, change coverage, or request a dedicated reference/previs rather than repeating an overloaded prompt.

## Failure guards

- Avoid “dynamic action” without path, force, phase and result.
- Avoid simultaneous hero action, intricate camera travel, dialogue and VFX unless timing proves each remains readable.
- Avoid impossible body support, prop ownership or collision response.
- Avoid reset after impact; preserve damage, debris, wetness, fatigue and recovery.
