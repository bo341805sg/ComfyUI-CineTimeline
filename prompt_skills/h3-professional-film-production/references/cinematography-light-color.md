# Cinematography, light and color

Make every visual choice answer an audience responsibility.

## Shot design

Choose framing and viewpoint to establish geography, reveal information, read performance, protect action/contact, express relationship or create an edit interface. Specify:

- subject priority and composition geometry;
- frame size, camera height, angle, side of axis and subject distance;
- foreground/depth layers, negative space, headroom and look/action room;
- perspective/lens character rather than unsupported brand names;
- focus responsibility and depth-of-field behavior;
- camera start, trigger, path/hold, speed, reframe, end frame and settle;
- motion texture: locked, restrained support, handheld observation or deliberately unstable.

Separate actor motion from camera motion. Use one dominant camera response per phase. Static is a positive choice.

## Optics and image behavior

Describe observable consequences: spatial compression/expansion, facial perspective, edge distortion, focus falloff, flare/bloom, motion blur, shutter feel, stabilization and highlight response. Avoid incompatible combinations and numerical lens/exposure telemetry unless a tested downstream component uses it.

Do not use shallow depth of field when hands, contact, props or geography must remain legible. Do not make focal changes compete with the dramatic action.

## Motivated lighting

Design from source to effect:

```text
source/motivation -> direction -> size/hardness -> level/ratio -> color -> subject/material response -> change during beat
```

Specify key, fill/negative fill, practicals, rim/separation, background depth, eye visibility and continuity. Preserve source direction across reverse coverage unless a motivated change occurs.

Use light changes caused by story events: door opens, vehicle passes, cloud clears, screen turns on, fire flickers. Avoid “cinematic lighting” without a source and responsibility.

## Color and material system

Define:

- dominant/secondary/accent relationships;
- value hierarchy and skin/subject separation;
- saturation and contrast strategy;
- color-temperature relationships;
- material-specific response for skin, hair, fabric, metal, glass, water and atmosphere;
- planned progression across segments;
- finishing restraint such as grain, halation, highlight roll-off or black floor.

Color progression must follow narrative change, not random shot variation. Keep identity-critical costume and product colors stable under changing light.

## Camera-performance coordination

Do not move or refocus through the only micro-expression, line delivery, hand contact or prop reveal unless movement is the intended observation. Protect anticipation, decisive moment, reaction and settle. Choose the lowest camera complexity that communicates the beat.

## Failure guards

- Avoid prestige adjective stacks without executable consequences.
- Avoid contradictory camera paths, simultaneous reframing responsibilities or axis-breaking drift.
- Avoid lighting every surface equally; establish hierarchy.
- Avoid crushed facial information, clipped highlights or color casts that destroy identity anchors unless intentional.
- Avoid unmotivated style changes between adjacent segments.
